Pattern library · educational reference

Price-action pattern playbook

Patterns are possible structures, not guarantees. The same shape can fail or mean different things depending on trend, location, session, volatility and higher-timeframe context. Use confirmation and predefined invalidation rather than assuming a pattern will predict the next move.

Choose a context, pattern and higher-timeframe alignment to create a checklist. This is a structured scenario prompt, not an automatic prediction.

1. Trend pullback

Look for a sequence of higher highs/higher lows (or lower lows/lower highs), then a pullback toward a prior structure level. A possible continuation requires the pullback to hold and price to show renewed momentum. Invalidation: the structure level that would break the trend idea.

2. Double top / double bottom

Two similar swing extremes may indicate a failed attempt to continue, but the pattern is not confirmed just because two peaks or troughs look alike. Watch the neckline/structure break and whether a retest holds. Invalidation is beyond the relevant swing extreme.

3. Breakout and retest

A close beyond a clearly marked level is more meaningful than a wick alone. A retest may hold, fail or never occur. Consider spread and news volatility. Invalidation: price returns through the level and breaks the structure supporting the idea.

4. Flag / pennant

A pause after a sharp move can resolve either way. Avoid calling every sideways cluster a flag. Look for a clear impulse, compact consolidation, and a break with follow-through; define the stop where the setup is invalidated.

Pattern-to-plan checklist

H4

Mark broad structure, major swing highs/lows and obvious support/resistance.

H1

Check whether intraday structure agrees, conflicts or is ranging.

M15

Define the setup level and what confirmation would look like.

M5

Use only for trigger timing after context is clear; do not let noise override H4/H1.

Candlestick names such as engulfing candles and pin bars are weak in isolation. Location, trend, volatility and a testable rule matter more than the label.

Study notes

Research principles to test, not blindly copy

These are high-level study prompts inspired by the requested books and trader biographies, paraphrased rather than quoted. They are not trade signals or claims that a method will work for you.

Market Wizards / The New Market Wizards

  • Write rules clearly enough to test.
  • Protect capital; losses are part of uncertainty.
  • Keep records and study your own behaviour.
  • Do not assume one trader's method transfers to every market.

Paul Tudor Jones · George Soros

  • Study risk control, asymmetric payoff and changing conditions.
  • Define the point where the thesis is wrong.
  • Do not confuse conviction with evidence.

Andrew Krieger · Ray Dalio

  • Understand leverage and tail-risk before sizing.
  • Separate macro thesis from trade trigger.
  • Check assumptions and alternate scenarios.

Joe Lewis · Bruce Kovner · Michael Marcus

  • Study patience, preparation, adaptability and risk discipline.
  • Review differences between plan and outcome.
  • Biographies are not ready-made strategies.

Research checklist

  • State the hypothesis and invalidation conditions.
  • Account for spread, commission and slippage.
  • Separate development from out-of-sample data.
  • Record eligible signals, including skipped trades.
  • Review drawdown and losing streaks, not just win rate.
